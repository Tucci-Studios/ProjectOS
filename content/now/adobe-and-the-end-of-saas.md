Wall Street has decided AI threatens subscription software. Adobe is down about 30% over a year. A single plugin launch in February set off a selloff worth around $300 billion across software stocks.

The thing being priced is a story: with AI, people will build their own tools instead of paying. Is it true?

**The small stuff: yes.** The credible first-hand accounts are people replacing $10 to $40 a month single-purpose tools in an afternoon or a weekend. A testimonial widget. A dictation app. A time tracker. One well-known engineer rebuilt his use of a $120-a-year tool in 20 minutes and was careful to say he rebuilt only the slice he used.

**The big stuff: no, not yet.** Nobody credible has replaced Photoshop for daily professional work. The viral open-source Adobe clone reports roughly 30% feature parity. Adobe itself has beaten earnings five quarters running. The honest consensus is that AI "exposes the middle": cheap tools aren't worth rebuilding, deep ones survive, and the $200-a-month single-feature tool in between is in trouble.

**The cost nobody tweets about.** Building is cheap now. Owning is not. Tokens expire, APIs change, operating systems update. One writer rebuilt a social-feed tool with AI and then went back to paying for it. That's the half of the story worth knowing before you cancel anything.
