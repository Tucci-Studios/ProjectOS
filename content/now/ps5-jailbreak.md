At the end of September a jailbreak called Relapse went public. It works on PS5 firmware from 7.00 up to 13.60, which is nearly every console that hasn't taken the September 16 update. It's tethered, meaning you re-run it after each restart, and it lets the console run software Sony didn't approve.

Within two weeks someone used it to get a PS2 emulator running on the PS5, playing original PS2 discs with 4K upscaling. Sony never offered that. The developer says PS3 discs are next, and lists Claude as a contributor to the code.

The part that makes this a story rather than a hobbyist footnote is what Sony said in August. Facing a class action, Sony's filing argued that buyers of digital games are licensing them, not buying them. The jailbreak coverage has been framed against that line ever since.

**What this is not.** It is not legal to use in the United States. Copyright law's anti-circumvention rules have no exemption for game consoles, and piracy through "backups" is straightforwardly illegal. Sony can and does ban consoles from its network. We don't host the exploit and won't link to downloads. We think you should know it exists, what it does, and why people are cheering.
